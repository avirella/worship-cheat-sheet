Common worship progressions

These cover a large share of modern worship songs. The numbers are scale degrees, so the same pattern works in any key. The 1 is the chord named for the key, and an m means minor. In G, that makes 1 = G, 4 = C, 5 = D, and 6m = Em.

Strumming patterns

D is a down strum, U is an up strum. Keep your hand moving even when you skip a stroke; it holds the groove steady.

Playing with a worship team

Song shape and team habits matter as much as the chords.

SectionWhat the guitar does
IntroSingle notes, arpeggios, or light pad chords
VerseFewer strums, quieter; let the vocal lead
Pre-chorusStart building with more strums
ChorusFull strumming with open voicings
BridgeThe peak: biggest dynamics, or a stripped-back moment
OutroLet it ring, then slow down or fade
  • Leave space. If there is a keys player, avoid doubling their chords; play higher voicings or use a capo.
  • Drop your volume when the congregation sings. You are there to support them.
  • Know the key and capo fret before rehearsal and write both on your chart.
  • Mark intros, tags, repeats, and transitions. Most mistakes happen between sections.
  • Keep your tone clean. Too much gain or reverb muddies the mix.
  • Choose the key for the singer's range first, then use the capo to keep your shapes easy.
  • Learn the 1, 4, 5, and 6m in G, D, C, A, and E. That covers most of what you will play.
